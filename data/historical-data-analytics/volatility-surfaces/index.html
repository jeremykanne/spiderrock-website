Historical Volatility Surfaces

SpiderRock operates servers that continually compute implied volatility surfaces for all option expiration months with live market quotes. These surfaces reflect a SpiderRock best fit of current markets and are subject to change if market conditions change. SpiderRock archives these surfaces at regular intervals for historical analysis for all options on stocks, ETFs, and options on futures indexes.

Surface Types

Volatility Surface Datasets

Live Surface Curve

This table contains spline curve parameters, implied volatility surfaces, Greeks, and hedge deltas for all options expiration months with live market quotes. The records backing each SpiderRock surface represent a two-dimensional curve with strikes on the X-axis expressed in terms of moneyness, and options volatilities on the Y-axis expressed as a multiple of at-the-money volatility.

Live Surface Grid Records (Skew Surfaces)

This table contains a fixed grid of volatility for a given expiration as a function of delta for uncensored curves. The file also includes days until the next earnings date and implied earnings moves.

Live Surface Fixed Term

This dataset contains snapshots of the censored at-the-money volatility term structure with the impact of implied earnings events removed for each ticker. Censored curves are created by eliminating the earnings volatility from the observed fitted curve, which enables the user to better understand the ticker’s underlying volatility.

Each snapshot contains at-the-money volatilities for fixed-term expirations from 5 days out to 504 days (two years). The data also includes historical earnings move history, estimated implied earnings moves, uncensored curves with earnings events included, and censored curves with the impact removed.

Use Cases

Why Use Our Historical Options Data With Volatility?

There are many use cases for volatility surface data and volatility term structure data. Accurate options pricing makes up the basis for valuation and risk calculations that are the foundations of strategy modeling, portfolio risk management, portfolio valuation, and return calculations.

A better understanding of a strike’s surface price allows for the valuation of those options more accurately and consistently. Compared to simple surface fits, like averaging prices for mid-market or taking the bid or ask as a closing price, SpiderRock surface pricing results in more accurate valuations and risk calculations. Over time, better valuations from surfaces can lower standard deviations and enable potentially better quantitative models.

Common Applications

  • Trading strategy development and portfolio valuation
  • Evaluating portfolio risk, including upside/downside risk via skew
  • Modeling short-term volatility markets at the individual strike level
  • Modeling volatility over time, implied earnings moves, market skew ahead of events, and implied market borrow rate
